**Nickel** is the name of the software your Kobo shipped with — the home screen,
the store, the reader. **KOReader** is a separate reading app you can
[install alongside it](/devices/kobo/libra-colour/koreader/) without removing
anything. Installing KOReader does not replace Nickel; it adds an icon that
switches to KOReader, and closing KOReader drops you back into Nickel.

So the real question is not "which one" — it is "which one for what".

## What Nickel does better

- **The Kobo store and library borrowing.** Buying books, and OverDrive /
  Libby library loans, only work in Nickel. KOReader cannot open a DRM-locked
  store or library book.
- **Kobo Plus and Pocket.** The subscription catalogue and the read-it-later
  integration are Nickel-only.
- **It just works.** No setup, no dense menus. Sync with your Kobo account,
  reading stats in the Kobo app, sleep-screen covers.
- **Fewer sharp edges.** It cannot really be misconfigured.

## What KOReader does better

- **Typography.** Real control over margins, line spacing, hyphenation,
  font weight, and per-font rendering. Nickel exposes a fraction of this, and
  hides some of it behind its own patched fonts.
- **Formats.** CBZ, CBR, FB2, DjVu, and a genuinely usable PDF mode with crop
  and column support. Nickel's PDF and CBZ handling is poor.
- **Custom dictionaries, Wallabag, RSS, OPDS.** Built in.
- **Progress sync that is not tied to a store.** The same reading position on a
  Kobo, a phone and a Kindle, through KOReader's own sync.
- **Statistics you own.** Detailed reading-time tracking stored on the device.

## What Nickel does that KOReader can be made to do

Nickel's own weaknesses — a cluttered home screen, no folder view, awkward
collections — are mostly fixable with the [Kobo mods](/guides/best-kobo-plugins/)
(NickelMenu and friends) rather than by switching to KOReader. If your complaint
is the home screen, not the reading experience, that is the lighter path.

## How most people settle it

Read **store and library books in Nickel**, because you have no choice, and read
**your own EPUBs, PDFs and comics in KOReader**, because it is better at them.
The KOReader icon on the Nickel home screen makes flipping between them a single
tap. Set KOReader's progress sync so a book you start in one carries over if you
open it in the other on another device.

If you only ever read Kobo-store purchases, KOReader has little to offer you.
If you sideload most of your books, it is the reason to own a Kobo rather than a
Kindle.
