Every reader has tap zones. KOReader has a full gesture manager: you can assign
any action — page turn, bookmark, dictionary, screen refresh, toggle status bar,
jump to a chapter — to a tap in a screen region, a swipe from an edge, a diagonal
swipe, or a "multiswipe" shape drawn with one finger.

## Where it lives

Tap the top of the screen for the menu, then the gear icon, then **Taps and
gestures**, then **Gesture manager**. Actions are grouped by where they apply:
the reader, the file browser, and everywhere.

## The defaults worth knowing

- **Tap** left third to go back a page, right two-thirds to go forward. Tap the
  top-centre strip for the menu.
- **Swipe** left or right anywhere to turn pages — more reliable one-handed than
  aiming for a tap zone.
- **Swipe down from the top** opens quick settings (brightness, warmth, refresh).
- **Swipe up from the bottom** opens the "bottom menu" with font and layout
  controls.
- **Diagonal swipe** from a corner: by default the bottom-left/right corners
  toggle the status bar and do a full refresh.
- **Two-finger tap** toggles the status bar; **two-finger swipe** adjusts
  brightness.

## Custom gestures most people set

- **Corner taps for chapters.** Bind top-left to "previous chapter" and
  top-right to "next chapter" so you can skip around without opening the menu.
- **A multiswipe for the bookmark list.** Draw an "L" or a "⌐" shape and bind it
  to "toggle bookmarks" or "show bookmarks".
- **A gesture for the dictionary.** If you look words up a lot, a short swipe or
  a corner tap set to "show dictionary" beats the long-press each time.
- **Full refresh on a swipe.** On devices that ghost, a bottom-edge swipe bound
  to "full screen refresh" is quicker than the quick-settings panel.
- **Toggle bookends / status bar.** If you use the progress overlay, put its
  toggle on a two-finger tap so you can hide it for a clean page.

## Physical buttons

If your device has page-turn buttons, they are configured in the same place
under **Physical buttons** (or "Key remap"). KOReader ignores the stock reader's
button settings and uses its own — long-press actions included.

## The short version

Learn the swipe-to-turn and the two swipe menus first. Then set corner taps for
chapter jumps and one multiswipe for bookmarks, and you rarely open the menu
while reading.
