On a Kobo, KOReader is a fifteen-minute sideload. On a Kindle it is not: you have
to [jailbreak the device](/guides/koreader-on-kindle-2026/), and then keep it on
old firmware forever, because an update removes the jailbreak. That cost changes
the calculation, so weigh it before you start.

## What KOReader gives you over Amazon's reader

- **Typography.** Margins, line spacing, hyphenation, font weight and embedded
  fonts, all adjustable. Amazon's reader has improved but still decides a lot for
  you.
- **Fonts without limits.** Any `.ttf` or `.otf`, not Amazon's shortlist.
- **Formats.** EPUB without conversion, plus CBZ, CBR, FB2, DjVu and a real PDF
  mode. The Kindle reader wants everything as a Kindle format and handles PDFs
  badly.
- **Store-free progress sync.** The same position across a Kindle, a Kobo and a
  phone, through KOReader's own sync rather than Whispersync.
- **Custom dictionaries, Wallabag, RSS, OPDS.** Built in.
- **No ads, no store, no "also bought".** KOReader is just the book.

## What you lose

- **Whispersync and the Kindle store.** KOReader cannot open a DRM-locked Kindle
  purchase or sync with Amazon. You keep the Kindle reader for those and switch
  to KOReader for your own files — the launcher makes it one action.
- **Automatic firmware updates.** You must block them. A Kindle that updates
  itself loses the jailbreak, and if it lands on firmware with no available
  jailbreak, KOReader is gone until the community releases a new method — which
  can take months.
- **Amazon support.** Not available for a jailbroken device.
- **Some risk.** The jailbreak itself is low-risk now (it runs in the Kindle's
  browser), but you are committing to managing the device carefully for as long
  as you want KOReader on it.

## Who should bother

- **Yes**, if you sideload most of your reading, care about how a page looks, and
  are comfortable keeping a device on locked-down firmware. A jailbroken
  Paperwhite running KOReader is a lovely reader.
- **Probably not**, if you mostly buy from the Kindle store, or you want a device
  you never have to think about. The stock reader plus
  [Send to Kindle](/devices/kindle/paperwhite/guides/send-to-kindle/) for the odd
  EPUB is the calmer choice.
- **Consider a Kobo instead.** If KOReader is the goal, a Kobo runs it with no
  jailbreak and no firmware anxiety. Many people who want KOReader on a Kindle
  would be happier having bought the other brand.

## The short version

The gains are real and the same as on any device. The Kindle-specific price is
permanent firmware management. If that trade sounds fine, do it; if it sounds
tiring, it will be.
