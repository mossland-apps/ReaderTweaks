steps:
  - instruction: Learn the default button behaviour
    detail: >-
      The two physical buttons sit on the wide bezel. Held in the right hand,
      the lower button turns the page forward and the upper button turns it
      back. The buttons work in any orientation; the device senses which way you
      are holding it and keeps "forward" as the button nearer your thumb.
  - instruction: Swap the buttons if forward and back feel wrong
    detail: >-
      Go to the home screen, open the menu, then Settings, then "Reading
      settings". Find the option for page-turn buttons and switch it to the
      reversed layout. This is the setting most left-handed readers change, and
      it also helps if you prefer the top button for forward.
  - instruction: Learn the tap zones
    detail: >-
      While reading, a tap on the right third of the screen turns the page
      forward, a tap on the left third turns it back, and a tap in the centre
      opens the reading menu. These zones work alongside the buttons, not
      instead of them.
  - instruction: Use swipes for finer control
    detail: >-
      Swipe left or right to turn pages. Swipe up from the bottom for the quick
      navigation bar and chapter list. Swipe down from the top for the
      brightness and warm-light sliders and the settings shortcut. A swipe from
      the left edge, if enabled, goes back a screen.
  - instruction: Adjust brightness with a button-and-swipe shortcut
    detail: >-
      Press and hold either page-turn button, then slide your thumb up or down
      the left edge of the screen to change the front-light brightness without
      opening any menu. This is a Kobo shortcut, not something you need to turn
      on.
  - instruction: Know what you cannot change
    detail: >-
      Kobo's stock software lets you reverse the buttons but not assign them to
      other actions such as bookmarks or notes. If you want fully
      remappable buttons, that is one of the reasons people install
      [KOReader](/devices/kobo/libra-colour/koreader/), which exposes every
      gesture and button as a configurable action.
---

The Libra Colour is built around one-handed reading, so it is worth a few
minutes to know what each control does. The buttons and the touch screen work
together: you can turn pages with either, and the reading menu, navigation bar
and light controls each have their own edge to swipe from. The one adjustment
most people make is reversing the buttons, which lives under Reading settings.
