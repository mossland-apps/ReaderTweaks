  - instruction: Use Send to Kindle for EPUB, DOCX and text
    detail: >-
      Install the Send to Kindle app on your phone or computer, or use the Send

      choose the Scribe. EPUB files are converted to Amazon's format on delivery;
      the text and chapters survive well, custom layouts less so.
  - instruction: Or email files to the Scribe's address
    detail: >-
      Every Kindle has an address like "yourname_123@kindle.com", shown under

    warning: >-

  - instruction: Understand how PDFs behave on the Scribe
    detail: >-
      PDFs are delivered as-is — no conversion — and keep their exact layout,
      which is the point of the big screen. They do not reflow: text size is
      fixed by the page, so a document typeset for A4 or Letter shows at close to
      full size on the 10.2-inch screen and is comfortable to read. Small-print
      or two-column PDFs still need pinch-zoom and panning.
  - instruction: Mark up a PDF with the pen
    detail: >-
      Open a PDF and start writing on it with the pen. The Scribe adds your
      handwriting as an annotation layer. You can highlight, add typed or
      handwritten sticky notes, and later export the annotated PDF by email from
      the share menu.
  - instruction: Keep originals in Calibre if you want a library
    detail: >-
      Send to Kindle does not organise anything or give you the converted file
      back. If you want a tidy catalogue and format control, keep your books in
      Calibre and send from there instead.

For EPUB and documents, Send to Kindle is the simplest route and works from a
phone. PDFs are where the Scribe earns its screen: they arrive unconverted, keep
their layout, and can be written on directly. The trade-off is that PDFs do not
reflow, so a document designed for a large page reads well while a dense
academic PDF still involves some zooming.
