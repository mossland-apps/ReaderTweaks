steps:
  - instruction: Gather the font files
    detail: >-
      You need files ending in ".ttf" or ".otf". A family usually comes as
      four — regular, bold, italic and bold-italic. Copy all four so books that
      use bold and italic display correctly.
    tip: >-
      The Kindle groups a family by the name inside the font file, not the file
      name, so you do not need to rename anything the way you do on a Kobo.
  - instruction: Connect the Paperwhite to your computer
    detail: >-
      Use the USB-C cable and choose to connect. The Kindle appears as a drive.
  - instruction: Open the "fonts" folder on the Kindle
    detail: >-
      At the top level of the Kindle drive there is a folder called "fonts",
      next to "documents". If it is missing, create it, spelled exactly that way
      in lower case.
    warning: >-
      Put the "fonts" folder at the root of the drive, not inside "documents" or

  - instruction: Copy your font files into the fonts folder
    detail: >-
      Drag the .ttf or .otf files in. Sub-folders are fine; the Kindle looks

  - instruction: Eject the Kindle and restart it
    detail: >-
      Eject the drive safely and unplug. Then restart the Kindle from Settings,
      then Device Options, then Restart. The font list is only rebuilt on a
      restart, so this step is not optional.
  - instruction: Select the font while reading
    detail: >-
      Open a book, tap the top of the screen, and open the font settings (the
      "Aa" button), then the "Font" tab. Your added fonts appear below Amazon's
      built-in ones. Note that custom fonts only apply to books in Amazon's
      formats; they do not change how PDFs look.

Kindles have quietly supported user fonts for years, and it works on the current
Paperwhite without any jailbreak. The process is the same idea as Kobo — copy
files into a "fonts" folder — with two differences: you do not need to rename
the files, and you must restart the Kindle afterwards for the new fonts to
appear.
