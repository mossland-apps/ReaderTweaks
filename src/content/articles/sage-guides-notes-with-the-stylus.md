steps:
  - instruction: Understand the two notebook types
    detail: >-
      From the home screen, open "My Notebooks" and tap the plus. A "Basic"
      notebook is a plain writing canvas — good for quick sketches and notes. An
      "Advanced" notebook is lined and supports handwriting-to-text conversion
      and simple layout tools, but is slower and more restrictive. Most people
      use Basic.
  - instruction: Learn the writing tools
    detail: >-
      The toolbar has a pen with a few thicknesses, a highlighter, an eraser, and
      undo and redo. There is no lasso-select, no layers, and no brush variety —
      it is deliberately simple.
  - instruction: Write notes inside a book
    detail: >-
      While reading, tap the top of the screen for the reading menu and choose
      the notebook or annotation icon. You can attach a page of handwriting to a
      spot in the book. These notes are tied to that book and sync with your
      reading position.
  - instruction: Convert handwriting to text
    detail: >-
      In an Advanced notebook, use the "Convert" option to turn a page of
      handwriting into typed text. It works best with clear printing. Basic
      notebooks do not offer conversion.
  - instruction: Export a notebook
    detail: >-
      Open a notebook, then its menu, and choose to email it as a PDF, or sync it
      to Dropbox or Google Drive if you have linked one under Settings. There is
      no way to pull the raw notebook file off by cable.
  - instruction: Mark up a PDF
    detail: >-
      Open a PDF and write on it directly with the stylus. The Sage keeps your
      marks as an annotation layer and can export the annotated PDF from the
      share menu. On the 8-inch screen this is workable for reports and sheet
      music, less so for dense A4 documents.
  - instruction: Set realistic expectations
    detail: >-
      The Sage is a reader that also takes notes, not a writing tablet. If
      note-taking is your main use, the Kindle Scribe or a reMarkable will do it
      better; the Sage's advantage is that it is also a proper Kobo e-reader.
---

The Sage's note-taking is intentionally basic: two notebook types, a handful of
tools, and export by email or cloud rather than an open file. That is enough for
meeting notes, book annotations and light PDF markup. If you need lasso-select,
layers, templates or serious handwriting search, this is not the device for it —
but as a reader that happens to take notes, it works well.
