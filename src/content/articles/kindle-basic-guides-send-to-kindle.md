steps:
  - instruction: Understand what Send to Kindle does
    detail: >-
      Send to Kindle takes a file from you and delivers it to your Kindle library
      over Wi-Fi, the same way a purchase arrives. It accepts EPUB, PDF, DOCX,
      RTF, TXT and common image formats. EPUB files are converted to Amazon's own
      format on the way; the text and chapters survive well, very custom layouts
      less so.
  - instruction: Pick a method
    detail: >-
      There are three, all free. The phone app is easiest if the book is on your
      phone. The website works from any computer. Email is best when another
      program, such as Calibre, can send files for you.
  - instruction: Method A — the Send to Kindle app
    detail: >-
      Install the Send to Kindle app on your phone or the desktop app on Windows
      or macOS. Sign in with your Amazon account. Drag a file onto the desktop
      app, or use "Share" then "Send to Kindle" on your phone. Choose the Kindle
      and send. It arrives in a minute or two if the Kindle is on Wi-Fi.
  - instruction: Method B — the website
    detail: >-
      Go to the Send to Kindle web page, sign in, and drag your files onto it.
      Select which devices should receive them and confirm.
  - instruction: Method C — email
    detail: >-
      Every Kindle has its own address like "yourname_123@kindle.com", shown
      under Settings, then "Your Account", or in "Manage Your Content and
      Devices". Email your files as attachments to that address.
    warning: >-
      Amazon only accepts email from addresses on your "Approved Personal Document
      E-mail List". Add the address you will send from in "Manage Your Content and
      Devices" under Preferences, or the message is silently dropped.
  - instruction: Wait for delivery and open the book
    detail: >-
      Make sure the Kindle has Wi-Fi on. New items appear in the Library, usually
      under "All" rather than "Downloaded", within a couple of minutes. If
      nothing arrives, check the file is under the size limit and that the sender
      address was approved.
  - instruction: Watch the storage
    detail: >-
      The entry Kindle has 16 GB, which is plenty for text books but fills faster
      with large PDFs and image-heavy files. Remove downloads you have finished
      from the device (long-press, "Remove download") to free space; the book
      stays in your account.
---

Send to Kindle is the least fussy way to read your own EPUB books on the entry
Kindle. There is no cable and no conversion software to install — Amazon does the
conversion during delivery. The two things that trip people up are the
approved-sender list for the email method, and expecting the file to look
identical afterwards; simple novels convert cleanly, heavily designed books less
so.
