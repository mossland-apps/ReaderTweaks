steps:
  - instruction: Make a notebook in BOOX Notes
    detail: >-
      Open the Notes app, tap to create a notebook, name it and pick a template —
      plain, lined, several grid and dot spacings, planners, Cornell, music
      staves and more. Different pages in one notebook can use different
      templates, and you can change a template later.
  - instruction: Learn the pen tools
    detail: >-
      The toolbar has pen, pencil, brush, highlighter and eraser, each with
      several widths, plus shape tools, a lasso to move or resize handwriting,
      undo and redo, and layers. Colours are available and show as muted tones on
      the Kaleido screen. The pen's side button can be set to erase.
  - instruction: Write on a PDF or a book page
    detail: >-
      Open a PDF in NeoReader and use the pen straight on the page — the marks
      are saved into a layer you can show or hide, and they stay at that spot.
      For reflowable EPUBs, the writing attaches to a location and moves with the
      text if you change the font size. You can also drop a collapsible sticky
      note.
  - instruction: Convert handwriting to text
    detail: >-
      In a notebook, use the "convert to text" option (whole page or lasso a
      selection). The device turns tidy handwriting into typed text you can copy
      or export. Printing converts better than fast cursive; several languages
      are supported.
  - instruction: Get notes off the device
    detail: >-
      From a notebook's menu, export as PDF (handwriting as-is) or as text, to
      local storage, the BOOX cloud, or a linked third-party cloud. Unlike a
      closed device, you can also just connect USB and copy the exported files
      off. The working notebook format itself is BOOX's own.
  - instruction: Consider a third-party notes app
    detail: >-
      Because it is Android, you can install OneNote, Xodo, or a dedicated note
      app from the Play Store and write with the pen in those. They lack BOOX's
      e-ink pen tuning, so BOOX Notes is usually smoother, but a third-party app
      is the way to keep notes in a system you already use.
  - instruction: Know the limits
    detail: >-
      You cannot import your own page templates as first-class templates (send
      them as a PDF and write on that instead). Handwriting search inside
      notebooks is limited. Colour on the page is muted — do not expect
      screen-accurate highlighter shades.
---

The Note Air 4 C's note-taking is genuinely good for meetings, margins and PDF
markup, with a large screen and a lag-free pen. It sits between the Kindle
Scribe and the reMarkable in polish, but beats both on openness: notes export as
PDF or text and you can copy the files off over USB, and you can run a
third-party note app if you prefer. The main limits are that the working
notebook format is BOOX's own and the template set, while large, is fixed.
