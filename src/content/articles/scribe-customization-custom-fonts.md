steps:
  - instruction: Gather the font files
    detail: >-
      You need files ending in ".ttf" or ".otf". A family usually comes as four —
      regular, bold, italic and bold-italic. Copy all four so books that use bold
      and italic display correctly.
    tip: >-
      Kindle groups a family by the name inside the font file, not the file name,
      so you do not need to rename anything.
  - instruction: Connect the Scribe to your computer
    detail: >-
      Use the USB-C cable and choose to connect. The Scribe appears as a drive.
  - instruction: Open the "fonts" folder
    detail: >-
      At the top level of the Scribe drive there is a "fonts" folder, next to
      "documents". If it is missing, create it, spelled exactly that way in lower
      case.
    warning: >-
      Put "fonts" at the root of the drive, not inside "documents" or another
      folder.
  - instruction: Copy your font files into it
    detail: >-
      Drag the .ttf or .otf files in. Sub-folders are fine.
  - instruction: Eject the Scribe and restart it
    detail: >-
      Eject the drive safely and unplug, then restart the Scribe from Settings,
      then Device Options, then Restart. The font list is only rebuilt on a
      restart.
  - instruction: Select the font while reading
    detail: >-
      Open a book, tap the top of the screen, open the font settings (the "Aa"
      button) and the "Font" tab. Your added fonts appear below Amazon's. Note
      that this changes books in Amazon's formats only — it does not affect how
      PDFs or your handwritten notebooks look.
---

Custom fonts on the Scribe work exactly as on the Paperwhite: a "fonts" folder
at the root of the drive, then a restart. The only Scribe-specific point is that
it applies to reading books, not to PDFs or notebooks, which keep their own
appearance.
