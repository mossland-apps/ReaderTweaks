steps:
  - instruction: Know what the Era can do that most readers cannot
    detail: >-
      It has two separate audio abilities and a real speaker. Text-to-speech
      reads a normal ebook aloud in a synthetic voice. Audiobook playback plays
      actual recorded audio files that you copy on yourself — MP3, M4A, M4B and
      OGG. Both can play out of the built-in speaker or over Bluetooth.
  - instruction: Use the built-in speaker, or pair headphones
    detail: >-
      For the speaker, you do not need to set anything up — audio just plays. For
      private listening, put your headphones into pairing mode, then on the Era
      open Settings, then Bluetooth, turn it on, and select them from the list.
      They reconnect automatically afterwards when both are on and in range.
    warning: >-
      Leaving Bluetooth on uses noticeably more battery. Turn it off when you go
      back to silent reading.
  - instruction: Set up a text-to-speech voice
    detail: >-
      Text-to-speech needs a voice package. Go to Settings, then "Language" or
      "Text-to-Speech", and download a voice for the language you read in. Some
      firmware includes one or two voices already. The download is over Wi-Fi and
      is a one-time step per language.
  - instruction: Read a book aloud
    detail: >-
      Open an ebook, tap the top centre for the reading menu, and choose the
      text-to-speech or "Voice" option. Playback controls and a reading-speed
      setting appear. The page turns itself as it reads, and it can keep going
      with the screen off.
  - instruction: Play your own audiobook files
    detail: >-
      Copy MP3, M4B or M4A files to the Era by USB, into the "Music" or "Books"
      folder. They appear in the Library alongside your ebooks. Open one to get a
      player with chapter navigation, a sleep timer and a playback-speed control.
    tip: >-
      M4B files keep their chapters and bookmarks; a folder of loose MP3 tracks
      is treated as one long file, so name the tracks in order.
  - instruction: Understand the limits
    detail: >-
      The synthetic text-to-speech voice is functional, not natural — it is for
      listening while doing something else, not a substitute for a produced
      audiobook. The speaker is small and mono, fine for spoken word but not
      music. And the Era cannot play DRM-protected audiobooks from Audible or a
      library app; only unprotected files.
---

Audio is the Era's clearest advantage over a Kobo or Kindle, and even over the
Verse Pro: it has a real speaker, so it will read your ebooks aloud and play
audiobook files with nothing else attached. Headphones over Bluetooth are there
for private listening. Both cost battery, and both are held back only by the
synthetic voice quality and the lack of support for DRM-locked audio.
