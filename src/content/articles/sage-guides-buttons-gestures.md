steps:
  - instruction: Learn the default button behaviour
    detail: >-
      The two physical buttons sit on the wide bezel. Held in the right hand, the
      lower button turns the page forward and the upper button turns it back. The
      device senses which way you are holding it and keeps "forward" as the
      button nearer your thumb, so the buttons work in any orientation.
  - instruction: Swap the buttons if forward and back feel wrong
    detail: >-
      Go to the home screen, open the menu, then Settings, then "Reading
      settings", and switch the page-turn buttons to the reversed layout. This is
      the setting most left-handed readers change, and it also helps if you
      prefer the top button for forward.
  - instruction: Learn the tap zones
    detail: >-
      While reading, a tap on the right side of the screen turns the page
      forward, a tap on the left turns it back, and a tap in the centre opens the
      reading menu. The zones work alongside the buttons.
  - instruction: Use swipes for chapter jumps and settings
    detail: >-
      Swipe left or right to turn pages. Swipe up from the bottom for the
      navigation bar and chapter list. Swipe down from the top for the brightness
      and warm-light sliders and the settings shortcut.
  - instruction: Adjust brightness with a button-and-slide shortcut
    detail: >-
      Press and hold either page-turn button, then slide your thumb up or down
      the left edge of the screen to change the front-light brightness without
      opening a menu.
  - instruction: Know the limit of the stock software
    detail: >-
      Kobo's software lets you reverse the buttons but not assign them to other
      actions. If you want fully configurable buttons and gestures, that is one
      of the reasons people install
      [KOReader](/devices/kobo/sage/koreader/).
---

The Sage is large enough that the buttons matter for one-handed reading. The
buttons and the touch zones work together, and the only change most people make
is reversing the buttons under Reading settings. The button-plus-edge-slide
shortcut for brightness is worth remembering on a screen this size.
