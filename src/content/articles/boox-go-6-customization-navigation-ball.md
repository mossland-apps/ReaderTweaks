steps:
  - instruction: Turn on the Navigation Ball
    detail: >-
      Open Settings, then "Navigation Ball" (sometimes under "Function
      settings"). Enable it. A small circular button floats over whatever app you
      are in; drag it to a corner out of the way of text. On the small Go 6
      screen, keep it near an edge so it does not cover words.
  - instruction: Choose the shortcuts
    detail: >-
      Tapping the ball fans out a ring of shortcuts. A useful set is: full-screen
      refresh, Back, Home, recent apps, and a brightness or refresh-mode toggle.
  - instruction: Set the full-refresh shortcut first
    detail: >-
      The single most useful shortcut is "refresh" or "full-screen refresh".
      Ghosting builds up in any app; a one-tap clear from the ball beats opening
      the control centre every time.
  - instruction: Set up edge-swipe gestures
    detail: >-
      Under Settings, then "Gesture", assign swipes from the left, right and
      bottom edges — for example swipe up from the bottom for recent apps or the
      control centre, swipe from the left edge for Back. Keep the page-turn area
      of your reading apps clear of these.
  - instruction: Check your reader's tap zones
    detail: >-
      Because there are no buttons, page turning is entirely touch. In each
      reading app, set generous tap-to-turn zones (or rely on swipes) so you are
      not aiming for a small target one-handed.
  - instruction: Hide the ball in full-screen apps
    detail: >-
      The ball has an auto-hide and opacity option, and you can toggle it off
      quickly from the control centre when you need the whole screen.
---

The Go 6 has no hardware keys, so the Navigation Ball and edge gestures are how
you move around Android without hunting for on-screen buttons that are hard to
see on e-ink. Set up a one-tap full-screen refresh first, add Back and Home, and
make sure each reading app's tap-to-turn zones are generous.
