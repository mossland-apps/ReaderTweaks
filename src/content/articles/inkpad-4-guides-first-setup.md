steps:
  - instruction: Charge the InkPad 4 before setup
    detail: >-
      A new device often ships part-charged, and setup includes a firmware
      update. Plug it in with the supplied USB-C cable and give it 30 minutes or
      more before you start.
  - instruction: Turn it on and choose your language and keyboard
    detail: >-
      Press the power button on the top edge. PocketBook asks for the interface
      language and keyboard layout first; this does not limit what languages your
      books can be in.
  - instruction: Connect to Wi-Fi and install any firmware update
    detail: >-
      Choose your network and enter the password. If an update is offered,
      install it and let the device restart. PocketBook updates add format
      support and fixes, so it is worth doing now.
  - instruction: Decide about a PocketBook account
    detail: >-
      A PocketBook account is used for the PocketBook Cloud (syncing books and
      reading position across devices and apps), the book store, and the
      Send-to-PocketBook email service. It is not required to read files you copy
      on yourself. You can skip it during setup and add it later under Settings,
      then Accounts and Synchronization.
    tip: >-
      If you plan to sideload everything by cable or use KOReader, skip the
      account. Add it only if you want the email delivery or cloud sync.
  - instruction: "Optional: authorise an Adobe ID for library books"
    detail: >-
      The InkPad 4 can open EPUB and PDF books protected with Adobe DRM — the
      kind most public libraries lend — without a computer. To use that, go to
      Settings, then Accounts and Synchronization, then "Adobe DRM", and sign in
      with a free Adobe ID. Skip this if you only read your own unprotected
      files.
  - instruction: Learn the bottom buttons and the SMARTlight
    detail: >-
      Two buttons on the bottom bezel turn pages; a short press turns forward or
      back, and you can change what a long press does later under Personalize.
      Swipe down from the top for the SMARTlight controls — brightness and a
      separate warm-tone slider — plus a shortcut into Settings. There is a
      g-sensor for automatic rotation, useful for landscape PDFs.
  - instruction: Add your books
    detail: >-
      Connect the InkPad 4 to a computer by USB and copy book files into any
      folder on the drive; the device indexes them on disconnect. Or use one of
      the wireless methods in the next guide. There is no microSD slot, so the
      32 GB of internal storage is all you have — still room for a very large
      library.

Setting up an InkPad 4 is quick. Two optional accounts are worth understanding:
a PocketBook account unlocks the wireless delivery and cloud features, and an
Adobe ID lets the device open DRM-protected library books directly. Neither is
needed just to read files you copy on by cable. The bottom buttons, the
SMARTlight and the auto-rotate sensor are the hardware features to get familiar
with early — the last one matters more here than on a small reader because of
wide PDFs.
