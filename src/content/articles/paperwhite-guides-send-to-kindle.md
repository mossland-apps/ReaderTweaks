steps:
  - instruction: Understand what Send to Kindle does
    detail: >-
      Send to Kindle takes a file from you and delivers it to your Kindle
      library over Wi-Fi, the same way a purchase arrives. It accepts EPUB, PDF,
      DOCX, RTF, TXT and common image formats. EPUB files are converted to
      Amazon's own format on the way; the text and chapters survive well, but
      very custom layouts and fixed-layout books may not.
  - instruction: Pick a method
    detail: >-
      There are three, all free and all tied to your Amazon account. The phone
      app is easiest if the book is on your phone. The website works from any
      computer. Email is best when another program, such as Calibre, can send
      files for you.
  - instruction: Method A — the Send to Kindle app
    detail: >-
      Install the Send to Kindle app on your phone or the desktop app on Windows
      or macOS. Sign in with your Amazon account. Drag a file onto the desktop
      app, or use "Share" then "Send to Kindle" on your phone. Choose the target
      Kindle and send. It arrives in a minute or two if the Kindle is on Wi-Fi.
  - instruction: Method B — the website
    detail: >-
      Go to the Send to Kindle web page, sign in, and drag your files onto it.
      Select which devices should receive them and confirm. This is handy on a
      work computer where you cannot install an app.
  - instruction: Method C — email
    detail: >-
      Every Kindle has its own address like "yourname_123@kindle.com". Find it
      under Settings, then "Your Account", on the device, or in "Manage Your
      Content and Devices" on Amazon's site. Email your files as attachments to
      that address.
    warning: >-
      Amazon only accepts email from addresses on your "Approved Personal
      Document E-mail List". Add the address you will send from in "Manage Your
      Content and Devices" under Preferences, or the message is silently
      dropped.
  - instruction: Wait for delivery and open the book
    detail: >-
      Make sure the Kindle has Wi-Fi on. New items appear in the Library,
      usually under "All" rather than "Downloaded", within a couple of minutes.
      If nothing arrives, check that the file is under the size limit (currently
      200 MB) and that the sender address was approved.
  - instruction: Optional — keep the original EPUB in Calibre
    detail: >-
      Send to Kindle does not give you the file back in Amazon's format, and it
      does not organise anything. If you want a tidy library and format control,
      keep your books in Calibre and use its own "Send to Kindle" or email
      feature instead.
---

Send to Kindle is the least fussy way to read your own EPUB books on a
Paperwhite. There is no cable and no conversion software to install — Amazon
does the conversion during delivery. The two things that trip people up are the
approved-sender list for the email method, and expecting the file to look
identical afterwards; simple novels convert cleanly, heavily designed books
less so.
