steps:
  - instruction: Understand where documents live
    detail: >-
      Everything on the reMarkable 2 syncs through your reMarkable account. You
      add a document in one place — the desktop app, the mobile app, email, a
      linked cloud folder, or the local USB interface — and it appears in your
      library. There is no plain USB drive to drag files onto.
  - instruction: Method A — the desktop app
    detail: >-
      Open the reMarkable app on your computer, sign in, and drag PDF or EPUB
      files into the library, or use "Import". They upload to your account and
      download to the device on its next sync. This is the main method for a
      batch of PDFs.
  - instruction: Method B — the mobile app and share sheet
    detail: >-
      On your phone, open a PDF and use "Share", then the reMarkable app, or add
      it inside the app. Good for a document that arrived by email on your phone.
  - instruction: Method C — email to your device
    detail: >-
      With a reMarkable account you can email documents to a personal address
      shown in the account settings. Attachments are added to your library.
      Availability depends on your plan.
  - instruction: Method D — the local USB web interface
    detail: >-
      The reMarkable 2 has a hidden local upload page. Connect it by USB, enable
      "USB web interface" in Settings, then open 10.11.99.1 in a browser
      on the computer and drag files onto that page. It works offline and does
      not touch your cloud sync — handy for a quick transfer or when Wi-Fi is
      awkward.
  - instruction: Method E — cloud integrations
    detail: >-
      With a reMarkable Connect subscription you can link Google Drive, Dropbox or
      OneNote and open files from them directly on the device. Without the
      subscription these are limited or unavailable.
  - instruction: Know the EPUB limitation
    detail: >-
      The reMarkable 2 opens EPUB, but its handling is basic — limited font and
      layout control, no library organisation to speak of, and some EPUBs render
      poorly. For serious ebook reading, a Kobo or Kindle is far better, or
      install KOReader. Use the stock reader for PDFs and the occasional EPUB you
      also want to mark up.
---

The reMarkable 2 has no plain USB file mode, but it does have more transfer
routes than the newer models: the apps, email, cloud links, and a local USB web
interface at 10.11.99.1 that works without touching cloud sync. The desktop app
is the workhorse for PDFs. Treat EPUB as secondary — the stock reader is weak,
and KOReader is the fix if EPUB reading matters to you.
