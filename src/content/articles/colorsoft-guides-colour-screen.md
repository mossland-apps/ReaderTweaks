  - instruction: Set your expectations for colour e-ink
    detail: >-
      The colour comes from a colour filter laid over an e-ink screen. Colours
      are soft and muted, closer to a printed newspaper than to a phone, and
      colour detail is half as sharp as text (150 ppi against 300 ppi). Black
      text looks as crisp as on a Paperwhite. This is normal for every colour
      e-ink reader, not a fault in yours.
  - instruction: Turn the light up a little
    detail: >-
      The front light shines through the colour filter, so colour looks richer
      with it on — even in daylight. Try a brightness around the middle of the
      slider when looking at covers or comics, and see the difference.
  - instruction: Use colour highlights
    detail: >-
      Select a passage, choose Highlight, and pick a colour. Amazon offers
      yellow, orange, blue, pink and green. Many readers give each colour a
      meaning — for example quotes in yellow and words to look up in blue. Your
      highlights and their colours appear in the book's Notebook.
  - instruction: Read comics and graphic novels
    detail: >-
      Comics bought from the Kindle store arrive ready to read in colour. For
      comic files you already own (CBZ or CBR), convert them first. The free
      Kindle Comic Converter is the common tool: pick a Colorsoft device profile
      if your version lists one, keep colour turned on, and send the result over
      USB or with Calibre.
    tip: >-
      Panel-by-panel viewing helps on a 7-inch screen. Double-tap a panel in a
      store comic to zoom to it.
  - instruction: Read colour PDFs and children's books
    detail: >-
      PDFs keep their layout and show charts and diagrams in colour, but a
      full-size page is small on a 7-inch screen, so expect to zoom. Picture
      books and children's books from the Kindle store display in colour as
      designed.

The Colorsoft's colour is real but gentle. It suits covers, highlights, comics,
picture books and the charts in a PDF, and it leaves black text exactly as sharp
as on the Paperwhite. The most useful single tip is to keep the front light on —
colour e-ink looks much better lit — and the most common disappointment is
expecting tablet-bright colours, which no e-ink screen produces.
