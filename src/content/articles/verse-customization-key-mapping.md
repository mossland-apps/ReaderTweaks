steps:
  - instruction: Open the key-mapping settings
    detail: >-
      From the home screen, open Settings, then "Personalize", then "Key mapping"
      (on some firmware it is under "Reading settings"). You will see a list of
      the physical controls the Verse has.
  - instruction: Understand short press and long press
    detail: >-
      Each of the two page-turn buttons has two actions you can set separately —
      a short press and a long press (holding the button for about a second).
      That gives you four assignable actions from two buttons.
  - instruction: Set the short presses for turning pages
    detail: >-
      Most people leave short press on the lower button as "Next page" and the
      upper button as "Previous page". To reverse them — common for left-handed
      readers, or if you prefer the top button for forward — swap the two here.
  - instruction: Assign the long presses to something useful
    detail: >-
      Long press is where the value is. Options usually include "Add bookmark",
      "Dictionary", "Screen refresh" (a full flash to clear ghosting),
      "Brightness", "Table of contents", "Rotate" and "Frontlight on/off". A
      common setup is long-press-down for bookmark and long-press-up for screen
      refresh.
  - instruction: Save and test
    detail: >-
      The changes take effect immediately. Open a book and try each press and
      hold to confirm they do what you expect. You can come back and adjust any
      time.
  - instruction: Note what carries over to KOReader
    detail: >-
      These mappings apply to PocketBook's own reader. KOReader has its own,
      separate and more extensive gesture and key settings, so if you run
      KOReader you will set its buttons up there instead.
---

Remappable buttons are one of the things the Verse does that a Kobo Clara or
basic Kindle does not. Two buttons with a short and long press each give you four
actions to assign. The short presses are usually best left as page turns (or
reversed), while the long presses are worth spending on bookmark, dictionary or
a manual screen refresh.
