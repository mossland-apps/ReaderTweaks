steps:
  - instruction: Understand what makes the InkPad 4 different
    detail: >-
      Unlike a Kobo or Kindle, the InkPad 4 opens most formats directly — EPUB,
      PDF, FB2, DjVu, DOCX, RTF, TXT, HTML, CBZ, CBR, MOBI and others. You almost
      never need to convert a file first. That makes copying books on the
      simplest method, and large PDFs and comic archives are exactly what this
      screen is for.
  - instruction: Method A — copy by USB cable
    detail: >-
      Connect the InkPad 4 to a computer with the USB-C cable and choose the
      connection prompt. It appears as a drive. Drag your book files into any
      folder, or into the "Books" folder to keep things tidy. Large PDF and CBZ
      files copy faster over USB than any wireless method.
  - instruction: Method B — Send-to-PocketBook by email
    detail: >-
      With a PocketBook account, the device has its own address such as
      "yourname@pbsync.com" (find it under Settings, then Accounts and
      Synchronization, then Send-to-PocketBook). Email your book files as
      attachments to that address. On the InkPad 4, open the "Send-to-PocketBook"
      folder and sync; the files download over Wi-Fi.
    tip: >-
      Email has an attachment size limit, so this suits ordinary ebooks better
      than a 300 MB scanned PDF — use USB for those.
  - instruction: Method C — sync a cloud folder
    detail: >-
      Under Settings, then Accounts and Synchronization, you can link Dropbox or
      Google Drive, or use the PocketBook Cloud. Put books in the linked folder
      from any computer, then sync on the device to pull them down.
  - instruction: Open a book and check the layout
    detail: >-
      For EPUB and similar reflowable formats, the reading menu (tap the top
      centre) has font, spacing and margin controls. For PDFs, the 7.8-inch
      screen shows a full page comfortably, but the dedicated PDF guide covers
      zoom, crop and reflow for anything that is still too small.
---

The InkPad 4's format range means "getting books on" is usually just copying a
file, and its screen makes big files worth carrying. Use USB for large PDFs and
comic archives, Send-to-PocketBook for ordinary ebooks from your phone, and
cloud sync to mirror a folder. Conversion rarely comes up here.
