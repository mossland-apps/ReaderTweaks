steps:
  - instruction: Know what the Verse Pro can do that most readers cannot
    detail: >-
      It has two separate audio abilities. Text-to-speech reads a normal ebook
      aloud in a synthetic voice. Audiobook playback plays actual recorded audio
      files that you copy on yourself — MP3, M4A, M4B and OGG. There is no
      speaker, so both need Bluetooth.
  - instruction: Pair your headphones
    detail: >-
      Put the headphones into pairing mode. On the Verse Pro, open Settings,
      then Bluetooth, turn it on, and select your headphones from the list.
      They reconnect automatically afterwards when both are on and in range.
    warning: >-
      Leaving Bluetooth on uses noticeably more battery. Turn it off when you go
      back to silent reading.
  - instruction: Set up a text-to-speech voice
    detail: >-
      Text-to-speech needs a voice package. Go to Settings, then "Language" or
      "Text-to-Speech", and download a voice for the language you read in. Some
      firmware includes one or two voices already. The download is over Wi-Fi and
      is a one-time step per language.
  - instruction: Read a book aloud
    detail: >-
      Open an ebook, tap the top centre for the reading menu, and choose the
      text-to-speech or "Voice" option. Playback controls and a reading-speed
      setting appear. The page turns itself as it reads, and it can keep going
      with the screen off.
  - instruction: Play your own audiobook files
    detail: >-
      Copy MP3, M4B or M4A files to the Verse Pro by USB, into the "Music" or
      "Books" folder. They appear in the Library alongside your ebooks. Open one
      to get a player with chapter navigation, a sleep timer and a
      playback-speed control.
    tip: >-
      M4B files keep their chapters and bookmarks; a folder of loose MP3 tracks
      is treated as one long file, so name the tracks in order.
  - instruction: Understand the limits
    detail: >-
      The synthetic text-to-speech voice is functional, not natural — it is for
      listening while doing something else, not a substitute for a produced
      audiobook. And the Verse Pro cannot play DRM-protected audiobooks from
      Audible or a library app; only unprotected files.
---

Audio is the Verse Pro's clearest advantage over a Kobo or Kindle: it will read
your ebooks aloud, and it will play audiobook files you own. Both go out over
Bluetooth, both cost battery, and both are held back only by the quality of the
synthetic voice and the lack of support for DRM-locked audio.
